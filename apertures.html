Matched apertures#

To model the same object at all wavelengths, the same aperture needs to be extracted (“matched aperture”). Depending on whether the source is extended or point-like, RainbowLasso uses either PSF-like photometry (weighted by the survey point-spread-function, best for stars and distant quasars) or a total flux based on an extraction aperture (for galaxies). The choice is made with the pointlike flag, taken from the Legacy Survey morphology.

Notes#

  • The VISTA/WFCAM aperture magnitudes follow the CASU aperture sequence (radii 0.5, 0.71, 1.0, 1.4, 2.0, 2.8, 4.0, 5.66 arcsec, steps of sqrt(2)): apermag4 is a 1.4’’ radius aperture (2.8’’ diameter) and apermag6 a 2.8’’ radius aperture (5.7’’ diameter). The VHS aperture sizes are documented in the VizieR II/367 column descriptions, and AperMag3 (1.0’’ radius) is the UKIDSS default aperture (II/319/las9).

  • The GALEX aperture magnitudes are stored with a 4 pixel radius (1.5’’ pixel, i.e. a 6’’ radius aperture); with its very large PSF, all objects are treated as point sources (see GALEX).

  • Euclid Q1 (optional) provides no aperture fluxes with suitable diameters; it only contributes an extended-fraction prior to the optical flux columns (see Euclid Q1 (optional)).

  • The extended-source apertures are chosen so that the same physical aperture is measured at all wavelengths as far as possible, given the resolutions above.
